## The visual mechanism

The board has a coherent grammar despite its mixed sources. Most designs take a small number of long curves, reflect them, then attach shorter return curls. The eye reads the negative space as a heart, vase, lyre, pointed oval or lozenge. That is why simply scattering decorative curls over a chocolate surface loses the character: the relationships between the curves are the design.

A straight perimeter gives each composition a stable silhouette. Within it, the major curve can lean, swell and cross. In the arched examples the top perimeter is already expressive, so the best translation leaves a calmer middle. The rectangle belongs to the whole frame. It need not become a repeated portioning grid inside the chocolate.

The most successful curves change radius gradually. They begin as long sweeps, tighten into a medium return and finish with a small terminal. Equal-sized curls everywhere make the field busy and flat. The Lyre reference is especially clear: the long outward sweep creates generous upper and lower chambers, while the constricted waist keeps them connected.

Symmetry supplies familiarity and visual stability, but there are several kinds. The red gate uses two related door leaves; the narrow grille uses one central vertical axis; the green panel uses both horizontal and vertical balance around a central crossing. These are different spatial structures. The library retains that distinction rather than turning every reference into the same mirrored floral stamp.

Crossings act as anchors. At the center of the heart-trellis panel, the diagonals make the diamond; an added diamond outline is unnecessary. The Penang door uses solid diamonds to mark intersections between soft ovals. The Indian-journey grille inverts that logic: concave arcs generate a four-point shape around a solid blue diamond. Those negative shapes remain readable when the small finish details are removed.

Repetition has two jobs in the references. In an actual grille it fills a protective screen; visually it establishes a predictable cadence. A small chocolate bar does not need the full number of repeats. The Four-point rhythm keeps two columns and three rows, with the intervening straight grid rails omitted. These are relief motifs on one unbroken face, not separate chocolate squares.

## The 4:6 translation

Each finished library frame uses a 60 × 90 mm working face, a 5 mm bar thickness, nominal 0.65 mm relief and a 0.35 mm bevel control. The aspect ratio is exactly 4:6. The physical size is a chosen editable starting point, not a measurement taken from the reference images.

The broad-relief SVGs use nominal 1.8–2.6 mm strokes and solid leaf/diamond forms. Their centerline study counterparts use a lighter 0.65 mm line to make the path structure easier to inspect. The study files are not the suggested mould face. Both versions keep the full composition inside one frame.

A01–A10 are grouped by structural mechanism, not country: paired C-scroll, lyre, heart trellis, four-point ogee, oval chain, twin hearts, arched portal, crowned gate, scroll vine and heart palmette. Each has source IDs and separate editable component groups in its project file.

The renderer turns the actual vector outlines into unioned, bevelled solid geometry. Overlapping relief is united before extrusion so crossings do not become separate wires. The exposed relief is attached to a continuous bar. The inverse preview forms an open negative cavity. Lighting, camera and material changes operate on that geometry, so rerendering does not invent or move motifs.

The library deliberately removes backing mesh, tiny twisted bindings, miniature spearheads, hardware, photographic corrosion and most tight tertiary curls. It enlarges the voids around return tips and reduces the number of repeats. Broad flat tops and a modest edge transition carry the pattern through reflected light; elaborate specular detail is unnecessary.

These settings are visual design starting points. The library does not claim that every gap or corner is manufacturing-approved. A tool maker must check draft, release, edge radius, minimum channel/gap, bar shrinkage, tool wall thickness and the chosen mould process before fabrication. The exported SVG is positive artwork, not a certified tool drawing.

## Provenance and cultural boundaries

The board mixes actual architectural objects, workshop examples, catalogue pages, sketches and reposts. It cannot establish one origin story for all 23 images. Some captions suggest South Asian or Southeast Asian settings; several catalogue and vintage examples draw on a wider ornamental ironwork vocabulary. Unlabelled pins remain geographically unverified.

For Azura, the useful connection to the earlier Omani door study is formal: symmetrical paired leaves, decorative metal scrolls, expressive negative space and a domestic threshold. The earlier 50/50 Omani/South Asian moodboard remains a separate sourced artifact. This new library should not be described as ten verified Omani designs.

Source images are preserved separately with original visible watermarks and details. The SVG files are clearly described as redraws/adaptations. The board is a research reference; it does not establish ownership or a licence for every underlying image or design.
